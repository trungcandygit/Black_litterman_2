## Required revisions

### RR1 (R1-W1, DA-C1): treatment defined with post-treatment information
R. The treated group is the August 2026 constituents, chosen on data from the treatment period; selection could generate the stage pattern.
A. We agree and confirmed the concern from FTSE's own FAQ: the final constituents were selected on data as of 30 June 2026. We now estimate intention-to-treat (ITT) effects for the 27 HOSE stocks on FTSE's preliminary list, screened on data as of 31 December 2024, before the announcement. ITT Amihud effects are -0.268, -0.554, -0.811 (all p < 0.01), 62% to 72% of the constituent effects; with never-named, never-included controls -0.286, -0.593, -0.860. Split: listed stocks later included -0.428, -0.872, -1.123; listed stocks not included -0.108, -0.245, -0.527 (last p < 0.05). Predicted constituents (top 27 by pre-period trading value): -0.250, -0.595, -0.741. The ITT group's announcement-week CAR is 7.5% (portfolio t = 3.97).
C. New Table 3 and text in Section 4.1; Section 3.1 (ITT group), 3.3 (ITT and predicted specifications), 3.4 (fourth assumption rewritten: "This assumption fails for the final constituents by construction ... The ITT group avoids the problem"); Section 5.2 closes with "We therefore read the constituent estimates as upper bounds ... and the ITT estimates as lower bounds." Abstract and Introduction report ITT magnitudes.

### RR2 (R2-W1, EIC-W2): control group contained named-eligible stocks
R. At least nine HOSE stocks on the preliminary list sat in the control group.
A. Confirmed: KBC, KDH, FRT, DGC, EIB, DPM, PDR, DIG and KDC passed all sample filters and were controls. We rebuilt all groups from the three public lists: 24 constituents, 14 named-but-excluded stocks, 328 never-named controls. Baseline Amihud estimates barely move (-0.392, -0.888, -1.129 vs -0.391, -0.884, -1.120 before), so the contamination did not drive the results. The naming-timing test now covers all 14 named-but-excluded stocks.
C. Section 3.1 (three groups), Table 1, Table 2 (comparison group relabelled "never-named"), Tables 6 to 8 re-estimated, Table 9 extended to 14 stocks, new Appendix Table A2 listing stocks by list.

### RR3 (R1-W2, DA-M1): CAR inference ignored event-date clustering
R. Cross-sectional t-statistics overstate precision when all stocks share the event date.
A. We now test every CAR on the equal-weighted group portfolio, with the standard deviation of daily portfolio abnormal returns over days -130 to -11 (Brown & Warner, 1985). Cross-sectional t-statistics were up to three times larger. Announcement [-1,+5] (t = 3.46) and confirmation [-1,+1] (t = 2.54) and [0,+20] (t = 2.11) remain significant; the constituent-list CARs no longer are (t = 1.82 for [-1,+1]).
C. Section 3.2 (test described), Table 4 (portfolio t in every cell, cross-sectional t shown for comparison), Section 4.2 rewritten; the constituent-list price claim is withdrawn from Abstract and Introduction.

### RR4 (R1-W3, DA-M1): benchmark size mismatch
R. An equal-weighted small-cap benchmark could turn large-cap market days into apparent FTSE effects.
A. Table 4 now reports four benchmarks: never-named equal-weighted, matched controls, large never-named stocks (top tercile of trading value), and a market model estimated per stock. Announcement [-1,+5] CARs range from 4.4% to 7.8% (t 2.16 to 3.46) and confirmation [-1,+1] from 2.9% to 4.5% (t 2.30 to 2.54). The announcement [-1,+1] CAR is not robust (t 0.93 with matched controls), so we now describe the announcement effect over the week [-1,+5].
C. Section 3.2, Table 4 panel A, Section 4.2 first paragraph.

### RR5 (R3-W1, DA-M2): effective-date claims must be scoped to the first tranche

A. Agreed. We now state the tranche schedule with its source and scope every effective-date statement to the first tranche.
C. Section 2.1 ("The effective date we study therefore carried only the first tenth of the eventual index weight"); Abstract ("the constituent list and the first 10% index tranche added no reliable price effect"); Section 4.3 last paragraph; Section 6 limitations.

### RR6 (R2-W2): use FTSE's screening cut-off dates

## Changes not requested but required by the new evidence
- The rebalancing test changed from a reference-day event regression to a spike test against the window baseline, because with the rebuilt matched set the reference session (20 August) was unusually active, which made earlier sessions appear significantly negative. The surge estimate is 0.79 to 0.84 (t about 6.8); named-but-excluded stocks show none (-0.01).
- The long-window CAR (announcement to 23 September) was removed because its inference also suffers from event-date clustering.
