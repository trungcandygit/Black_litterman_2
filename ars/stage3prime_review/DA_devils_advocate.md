### Strongest remaining counter-argument

The revision swaps one selected treatment group for another that is still defined by size, and it adds a bounding claim that leans on assumptions the paper never states. The 27 stocks on the preliminary list were the largest and most liquid on the exchange at the end of 2024, and the comparison group is 328 far smaller stocks. The paper's own "predicted constituents", chosen on pre-period trading value alone, show almost the same declines as the ITT (-0.25, -0.59, -0.74 against -0.27, -0.55, -0.81). The ITT therefore cannot tell an upgrade effect on likely constituents apart from any shock that favoured Vietnam's largest stocks over this period, such as the removal of foreign pre-funding, the bank and broker rally, or large-cap turnover growth. Neither the ITT nor the predicted group gets size-by-week fixed effects, randomization inference or a trend check, and the ITT's own event study rejects parallel pre-trends (F = 7.59) without being reported. Within the list, the stage pattern belongs to the 15 names FTSE later chose on June 2026 data. The 12 names it passed over barely moved until the list window (-0.11, -0.25, both insignificant), which is what selection on post-announcement outcomes predicts. For prices, the pre-determined group's confirmation CAR is insignificant at 5% under both benchmarks, so the "second price step" and the story that investors first priced eligibility and then inclusion rest on the post-selected group. The event-window change also carries weight: the three-day announcement effect does not survive size-appropriate benchmarks. The upper-bound and lower-bound reading needs a selection bias that is signed, one estimand shared by two different populations, and confounding common to both groups set at zero. None of these is shown. What the revision does establish is a large, ITT-robust liquidity divergence between Vietnam's largest stocks and the rest of HOSE, together with a rebalancing-day volume spike.

## New issues

NEW-1. MAJOR. Upper/lower-bound reading of constituent vs ITT estimates is not logically justified.
- location_anchor: [v3 L 358] "We therefore read the constituent estimates as upper bounds on the effect of the upgrade for included stocks and the ITT estimates as lower bounds"; [v3 L 372] "We report the constituent estimates as upper bounds and the intention-to-treat estimates as lower bounds; both show a large response at disclosure."
- description: The two estimates bracket one parameter only under assumptions the paper does not state or test. (1) Upper bound needs a non-negative selection bias. The paper itself names forces that bias the constituent estimate toward zero: anticipation [v3 L 113] and rising volatility, which enlarges the effect when controlled [v3 L 260]. The net sign is therefore unknown. (2) Lower bound for "included stocks" needs the effect on the 12 listed-but-excluded stocks to lie between zero and the effect on included stocks, and it needs the ITT to be free of non-selection bias. (3) The populations differ: the ITT includes 12 stocks that were never included and omits the 9 constituents that were not on the list [v3 L 398]. (4) Both estimates share the same comparison group and parallel-trends assumption against small caps (pre-trend tests reject for both: F = 6.66 and 7.59, output/revision/t_pretrend_wald.csv). A large-cap common shock would therefore push both in the same direction, and neither would bound it. As written, "bounds" gives the constituent numbers an identification status they do not have.
- attribution: regression. evidence: the bound language is absent from v2 (no "upper bound"/"lower bound" in manuscript_v2.md; the v2 counterpart [v2 L 310] says only that the checks "limit this concern").

- nearest_roadmap_item: RR1. non_match_rationale: RR1 requires the ITT to be run and reported beside the constituents with reconciled conclusions.
