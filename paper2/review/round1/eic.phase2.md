## Review Body

Editorial oversight view, framed against a generic Q1/Q2 empirical-finance field journal. No venue criteria are bound and none are claimed. Technical correctness of the standard errors, event construction and HOSE institutional facts is left to the methodology and domain seats; I rely on their findings for those questions.

The paper tests a pre-specified family of 26 hypotheses on 347 HOSE stocks over about 21 months. It finds that none of 22 price- and volume-based characteristics survives multiplicity control, while four price-limit event tests do. A post hoc decomposition then attributes the ceiling effect to an overnight gap followed by a partial intraday reversal, and a comparison with moves just below the limit is read as a discontinuity at the limit. The manuscript is unusually candid about its own status. It concedes that the close-to-close effect is already public in a non-peer-reviewed analysis, that the specification log is internal, and that the project was selected among three manuscripts and 14 ideas. This candour is the paper's main editorial strength. It also shows where the contribution is thin for a leading field journal.

On D6 I score warn rather than block. The work could reach a venue bar after substantial reframing, so it is not rejection-level. The part the abstract leads with, "4 of 4 survive", is the part the paper itself says is already public. The part that is new, the decomposition and the below-limit comparison, is labelled post hoc. The confirmatory label therefore supports the least novel result, and the novel result is exploratory by the authors' own account. The "pre-specified" status rests on an internal log written within a project whose earlier manuscripts used related data. It cannot be verified externally. The paper says this plainly, so I treat it as a framing and scope problem, not an integrity problem. My preliminary overall signal for the panel is Major Revision, resting on contribution and framing, and I note that this seat does not make the editorial decision.

On D5 I score warn. The manuscript is followable and well organised. Headline numbers trace to tables, and the pre-specified versus post hoc line is tabulated in Appendix A. The defects are repairable but real for a submission: declarations are still placeholders, key support is cited to internal repository files that readers cannot see, and the two abstracts disagree on the status claim.

### S1: Candid status disclosure and bounded novelty claim
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Section 1 "We do not claim to be first to document the close-to-close ceiling effect, nor its interpretation."
**Confidence**: 4 - editorial reading of the claims against the cited literature

The contribution statement is bounded to a search and states its overlap with prior public work. It also says the result is not an independent replication. Most submissions in this literature overstate priority. This one does not.

### S2: Pre-specified versus post hoc status is tabulated and the exploratory analyses are labelled throughout
**Evidence Anchor**: table: Appendix A (pre-specified versus post hoc status of each analysis family)
**Confidence**: 4 - presentation and traceability check across Sections 3 and 4

A reader can see which analyses were fixed before computation and which were added afterwards. The Table 3 to Table 7 event-count reconciliation is explained in the captions. Section 4.7 reports where results are benchmark-sensitive, for example the five-day tradable shortfall.

### S3: The investability question is answered in a way that is useful to the readership
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Section 4.6 "A long-only investor therefore gains nothing from chasing ceiling stocks at the open"
**Confidence**: 3 - practitioner relevance judged by an editor, not a specialist

Separating the gap from the post-open return, and showing the effect is not capturable by an outside buyer, gives the paper a clear takeaway for practitioners and regulators.

### W1: The abstract's headline confirmatory result is the already-public part, while the new result is post hoc, and the confirmatory label cannot be verified externally
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Section 3.1 "it is not an external registry entry"
**Confidence**: 4 - assessment of framing against the paper's own disclosures

The abstract leads with the pre-specified result, "4 of 4 survive". The paper concedes that this close-to-close effect is already reported publicly. The decomposition and the discontinuity comparison that carry the new content are labelled post hoc (Appendix A). The specification is an internal time-stamped log, and the same project had already produced related-data manuscripts. A confirmatory label of this kind is unverifiable and not independent of the project's prior results. The authors should state in the abstract and introduction which finding is confirmatory, which is exploratory, and which of the two the contribution rests on. They should also drop or qualify "pre-specified" wherever it could be read as registered. Added evidence such as a longer or out-of-sample window would give more support than relabelling does.

### W2: Incremental contribution over concurrent public work and the U.S. overnight-reversal literature is modest for a leading field journal
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "our contributions are the overnight–intraday decomposition, the comparison with moves just below the limit, and the multiple-testing frame"
**Confidence**: 3 - novelty judged from the references the paper itself cites, without an independent literature search

The overnight gain followed by an intraday giveback is already documented by Berkman et al. (2012) for attention-grabbing stocks. The paper says so. The close-to-close ceiling effect, its mechanism and the non-capturability inference are already in the public analysis whose sample contains this window. What remains is the application of that signature to a price limit on one exchange, plus a below-limit comparison that the authors call an association and not a regression discontinuity. That is a real but modest increment. The paper must say plainly why the application to a limit adds knowledge beyond the U.S. result, for example by testing what the limit mechanism predicts and the U.S. mechanism does not. Alternatively, it should present itself as a focused market-structure note.

### W3: The characteristic-zoo null and the limit finding read as two loosely joined papers
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Section 5 "The limit result stands out because every other characteristic in the same family is null."
**Confidence**: 3 - structural judgement; the methodology seat assesses power

The introduction justifies the family-wide frame as an answer to the objection that a large effect found after a long search is luck. Section 5 then states that the limit hits and the characteristics are compatible but unrelated. The zoo half also has limited power by the paper's own Table 8, with minimum detectable slopes of 0.12 to 0.31 percentage points per week. It therefore supports a weak message on the characteristics. The limit half carries the entire contribution. A reader gets two messages, "characteristics are null" and "limits matter", with no stated link between them. The authors should either motivate the zoo as an explicit multiplicity device for the limit result and shrink it accordingly, or separate the two.

### W4: Generality and policy implications exceed a single-exchange, 21-month sample
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Limitations (x) "The sample is a single market regime on one exchange."
**Confidence**: 3 - scope judgement for an editor

Section 5 draws implications for regulators about whether the limit settles the price, and for investors. The paper does not verify the price-limit rule, tick sizes or rule changes against exchange documents. It also infers the unfilled-demand mechanism without order-book data. Both are acknowledged in the limitations. The implications paragraph should be worded as conditional on this market and window, and the regulatory claim should be softened to what the data show.

### W5: Declarations are placeholders and key support points to internal files unavailable to readers
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Declarations "To be completed by the authors."
**Confidence**: 5 - directly observable

Ethics, funding, competing interests and author contributions are unfinished. The specification, post hoc log, literature search log and a devil's-advocate review are cited by internal repository paths (`process/decisions.md`, `review/DA_checkpoint1_report.md`) that a journal reader cannot inspect. The only support for the closest prior result is a GitHub repository that is not peer reviewed. The AI-assistance declaration is specific, which is good, but it is paired with a statement that references still need verification before submission. The authors should complete the declarations. They should move what matters from internal logs into an appendix or archive, and say how the GitHub source can be cited durably.

### W6: The Vietnamese abstract describes the hypotheses as registered in advance, contradicting the English text
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Tóm tắt "một họ 26 giả thuyết đã đăng ký trước"
**Confidence**: 3 - limited reading of Vietnamese; the phrase appears to mean "registered in advance"

The English abstract and Section 3.1 say that the specification was fixed in an internal log and was not an external registry entry. The Vietnamese abstract uses wording that reads as registered in advance, and it omits the caveat about the earlier-published public analysis. The two abstracts should say the same thing about the status of the test family.

Further presentation points, not scored as findings. Each table caption appears twice, once above and once below the table. The English abstract is long and carries eleven numeric results, which blurs the single message. The figures were not rendered for this review, so I assess the exhibits from their captions and tables only.
