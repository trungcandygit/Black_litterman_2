## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): from an editorial-oversight stance, this asks whether the study design, data handling, statistical reporting and reproducibility affordances reach the standard a peer-reviewed venue in the field expects. It is outside my scoring remit, but I note that a methodological failure here would limit what any fit or contribution judgment of mine can rest on.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat): this asks whether the claims agree with the current evidence in the subject area, whether earlier work is represented faithfully, and whether domain terminology and results are free of factual error. I do not score it. As editor I only rely on the domain seat to tell me whether the stated positioning against prior work is sound.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, shared by the devil's-advocate and methodology seats): this asks whether the central thesis is internally consistent, whether the evidence offered actually supports the claims made, and whether any fallacy undermines the main argument. It is not mine to score. I will read the other seats' findings on it when judging whether the paper delivers one message.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by the perspective seat): this asks whether framing, definitions and implications are accessible to readers from neighbouring fields and whether any interdisciplinary claims are backed up. I do not score it and defer to the perspective seat.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by me): this asks whether the manuscript is well organised, whether the exposition is clear, whether figures and tables are of good quality, and whether the paper follows the conventions of a journal in its field. As the Journal-Fit Reviewer I score this at the level of reader experience and editorial presentation, not technical correctness.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by me): this asks whether the manuscript suits the configured venue and makes an original, significant contribution appropriate to that venue's readership. Because no specific venue or criteria set is bound for this run, I judge fit against a generic leading empirical-field-journal bar and claim no alignment with any named venue's criteria. I treat the gap between what the paper claims and what it delivers as central to this dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: Whether title, abstract, introduction, body and conclusion tell one consistent story with a clearly stated question; whether sections are ordered so a reader can follow the argument; whether tables and figures are legible, labelled, self-contained and referenced from the text; whether headline numbers can be traced to a specific table or figure; whether declarations, disclosures and reference lists are complete, final and free of placeholders; whether length and form fit a generic top-field-journal submission.
what_triggers_block: The organisation or prose is so disordered, or the exhibits so unreadable or mismatched with the text, that an editor could not follow what was done or locate the support for the main results without rewriting the paper.
what_triggers_warn: The paper is followable but has repairable presentation defects, such as inconsistent claims between abstract and body, headline figures without a clear exhibit anchor, unfinished or placeholder declarations, unclear or overlong passages, or reference-list and formatting lapses.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: Whether the central contribution is stated plainly and is incremental, moderate or substantial relative to the closest prior literature the paper itself names; whether the source of novelty (new data, new method, new framing or new combination) is identifiable; whether the evidence base is broad enough for the generality of the claims made; whether the paper has one coherent message or is split into loosely joined parts; whether the stated status of the analysis (planned in advance versus exploratory) is supported by verifiable evidence and matches how it is used to frame the headline; whether the contribution statement and abstract match what survives scrutiny; whether the readership of a generic Q1/Q2 field journal would find it of interest.
what_triggers_block: The contribution is essentially already established in the literature the paper cites, or the headline claim rests on a framing (such as a pre-commitment or confirmatory label) that the evidence cannot support, so that no revision of presentation alone could make the paper suitable for a leading field journal.
what_triggers_warn: A real but modest contribution is overstated, the scope of claims exceeds the evidence base, the paper splits into parts that do not support one message, or the confirmatory-versus-exploratory line is unclear or unverifiable, yet the work could reach the venue bar after substantial reframing or added evidence.
what_triggers_fatal: The headline contribution is both non-original and misdescribed so that the paper's central claim is invalid as stated, for example a labelled-confirmatory result that is in fact post hoc and carries the main conclusion, or a core finding that merely replicates published work presented as new, leaving nothing of venue-level significance after correction.
