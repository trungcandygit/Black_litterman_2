# Response to the fourth set of comments (6 October 2026)

1. **Absolute literature claims; search strategy; unadjusted prices.** Every "none/no study" statement is now bounded to "the studies identified by our search", and a new Appendix B documents the search (dates, engine, query families, screening, inclusion rules, record-level reading). Unadjusted exchange data are not available to us. Instead, the event definition is shown to be independent of the tick grid: it uses only returns and the close-at-high (low) condition, which proportional adjustment leaves unchanged (Section 4, "Samples"), and Section 5 now reports tick-free definitions (returns >= 6.0%, >= 6.8%, within 6.7%-7.3%, without the close condition, and locked days), all with ceiling gaps of about 2.1% to 2.9% (table C28, `R/52_reviewer_round4.R`). The exact-hit split stays a diagnostic only.
2. **Sample sizes.** Section 4 now has a "Samples" paragraph that states, with inline counts from the saved tables, which complete-case requirement each exhibit uses: Table 1 (complete d+1..d+5 returns: 3,187 / 2,105), Tables 2-4 (day-d+1 open and close: 3,199 / 2,111), control benchmarks (at least 30 controls: 3,100 / 2,047), Figure 2 (events to the second-to-last day: 3,220).
3. **Causal language.** The abstract and conclusion now say the pattern is "consistent with" delayed price discovery and also with news, attention, and opening-auction overshooting. The title is descriptive and unchanged.
4. **Post-KRX concentration.** New Table 5 reports events and gaps by calendar quarter (the ceiling gap is positive and significant in all 8 quarters; the floor gap negative in all, significant in 7). Placebo breaks on the first trading day of each month (Jan 2025-Apr 2026) show that the KRX split gives the largest but still insignificant ceiling-gap difference, and that the floor gap changes gradually. A difference-in-differences regression against 5-6.5% movers at the extreme shows the excess ceiling gap of 2.91 pp (t = 6.2) before the change and an insignificant change after it (0.38 pp, t = 0.8) (tables C29-C31).
5. **Family of tests.** Section 4 explains why the 26 tests form one family (all candidate short-horizon predictors examined on the same data under the plan). Section 5 reports the event tests under six alternative family definitions (event tests only; ceiling and floor families; Holm; Bonferroni; 28 tests adding d+2..d+5; week-clustered event p-values); all four survive, with the largest adjusted p-value 0.0026 (table C32).

All new analyses are exploratory (post hoc) and were added to `R/run_all.R`.
