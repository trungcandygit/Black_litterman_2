# Literature review and benchmark: daily price limits, post-limit returns, overnight versus intraday returns (Paper C support)

Date of search: 2026-10-05. Mode: deep-research lit-review (bibliography_agent, source_verification_agent, synthesis). Scope: price limits and post-limit-hit returns; the four hypotheses; overnight/intraday returns; theory of limits and circuit breakers; multiple testing and Fama-MacBeth practice; Vietnamese microstructure.

## 0. Method, verification standard and limits of this review

- Tools: WebSearch only. WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every host tried (princeton.edu, ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, ideas.repec.org, plos.org, wiley.com), so no page could be opened. Verification is therefore at the level of "a bibliographic record (authors, journal, volume, pages, year) and/or abstract text was returned by the search tool". The search tool returns summaries it generates from the pages; where I quote, I quote the abstract text as shown in that record and label it "(search record)". No record was opened in full text. Nothing was read from memory except where flagged "carried from 04 log".
- Stage-1 database coverage is limited to what the web search reached (no Scopus/WoS). Novelty statements must be phrased "to our knowledge, among sources found by this search".
- DOIs are given only where the search record displayed one (in the record text or in the publisher URL); otherwise "no DOI seen".
- Evidence grade. The skill's 7-level hierarchy is built for clinical evidence; mapped here as: L-I = systematic or meta-research synthesis of the literature (multiple-testing meta-studies); L-III = quasi-experimental or regulatory-change design with a control or discontinuity; L-IV = large-sample observational/cohort-type panel or event study; L-VI = single descriptive/ simple-sample study; L-VII-T = theoretical model (foundational, no empirical weight). Grades are mine, from the design as described in the record.
- Journal tier (my classification, no ranking list was retrieved): T1 = Journal of Finance, JFE, RFS, AER, Journal of Econometrics, JFQA, Journal of Business; T2 = leading field journals (JBF, JEF, Journal of Financial Markets, Journal of Financial Research, Review of Finance); T3 = specialist Q1/Q2 (Pacific-Basin Finance Journal, Int. Rev. Financial Analysis, Finance Research Letters, Research in Int. Business and Finance, Emerging-market and regional journals, Physica A); T4 = mega-journal (PLOS ONE). Recency flag: R = 2018 or later; F = foundational (kept for theory, the four hypotheses, or as the originating paper).
- Magnitudes: only those visible in a record are given. The most important negative result: no peer-reviewed study retrieved reports a next-day or overnight/intraday magnitude after a limit-hit close that is measured the same way as ours (Section 2).

## 3. Thematic synthesis

Theme 1. Limits as delay, not protection: the four hypotheses. From Chen (1993) through Kim and Rhee (1997), Bildik and Gülay (2006) to Qi (2023), tests find support for volatility spillover, delayed price discovery and trading interference; the magnet effect is supported in Taiwan (Cho et al., 2003) and in the Shenzhen account data (Chen et al., 2019), rejected for ChiNext in Qi (2023). Evidence is mostly event studies on volatility, with few measures of the post-hit return itself. Spillover looks asymmetric (Aktas et al., 2020; Qi, 2023): lower-limit hits are costlier than upper-limit hits.

Theme 2. Band-change quasi-experiments (2018-2026). ChiNext 10% to 20% (Qi, 2023; Tang, 2023; Zhang et al., 2022; Jia et al., 2024), Taiwan 2015 (Lien et al., 2019) and Korea 2015 (Kim and Jun, 2019) give the strongest causal designs (L-III). Typical results: higher liquidity and volatility in China, with a higher probability of informed trading (Zhang et al., 2022) and lower crash risk through lower information asymmetry (Jia et al., 2024), and higher information asymmetry in Taiwan (Lien et al., 2019): effects depend on market and investor mix. (Corrected at Stage 4.5 round 3.) Vietnam's ±7% HOSE band has not (in this search) been tested in this way.

Theme 3. Who trades the limit, and what happens after: retail, attention and large investors. Chen et al. (2019) show large investors push to the ceiling and sell the next day, which links limit hits to short-horizon continuation and long-run reversal; Lin et al. (2023) and Zeng et al. (2024) price upper-limit exposure negatively in the cross-section; Tang (2023) and Jones et al. (2025) point to the retail/institution split. The attention mechanism of Berkman et al. (2012) is the same channel invoked for overnight returns.

Theme 4. The overnight-intraday decomposition as a lens on clienteles and liquidity provision. US evidence (Berkman et al., 2012; Aboody et al., 2018; Lou et al., 2019; Hendershott et al., 2020; Bogousslavsky, 2021; Akbas et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2023) shows overnight continuation, intraday reversal and a role for retail demand and market-maker inventory. Chinese evidence (Qiao and Dam, 2020; Chu and Song, 2023; Qiu et al., 2025; Gu et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2025) adds a T+1 and price-limit setting where overnight returns are often negative on average, which sharpens the contrast with a positive ceiling-day gap. No retrieved study decomposes post-limit-hit returns into overnight and intraday parts.

Theme 5. Inference discipline. A t-statistic above 3 or even 3.4-3.8 is advised for new cross-sectional effects (Harvey et al., 2016; Chordia et al., 2020; Harvey and Liu, 2020), and most anomalies fail replication with microcaps mitigated (Hou et al., 2020). For panels, Petersen (2009) frames how the Fama-MacBeth errors behave under time and firm dependence; weekly return panels have a documented reversal-then-momentum structure (Gutierrez and Kelley, 2008). Theory of limits (Brennan, 1986; Greenwald and Stein, 1991; Kodres and O'Brien, 1994; Subrahmanyam, 1994; Chen et al., 2024) rationalises both a protective and a perverse (magnet, advance-trading) role for limits.

### Gap our paper addresses (bounded by this search)
Among the sources found, none reports, for a retail-dominated frontier market with narrow limits, (i) the close-to-close continuation after ceiling and floor closes split into an overnight gap and an intraday reversal, (ii) with date-clustered, multiple-testing-controlled inference across a family of characteristics, and (iii) around the 5 May 2025 trading-system change. The nearest work is on China, Taiwan and Korea (limit-band changes and volatility) and on US/China overnight-intraday anomalies; Vietnamese price-limit evidence in this search was limited to unpublished or non-peer-reviewed items (4C). Novelty wording: "to our knowledge, among the sources found by this search."
